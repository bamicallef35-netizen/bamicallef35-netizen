### Ben Micallef

I build small machines whose behaviour comes out of a tight loop with their surroundings, using cheap current hardware and tests behind each claim. My company, [Sentinus](https://sentinus-labs.vercel.app), works on autonomy for vehicles that cannot rely on GPS.

I'm a DPhil candidate at Oxford, writing on cybernetics and avant-garde language. Before Oxford I did an MRes in architecture and digital theory at the Bartlett and a BA in philosophy and comparative literature at Berkeley, where I competed in NCAA Division I athletics. I also ran for Malta's national team.

**[umwelt-engine](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/umwelt-engine)**: a `no_std` Rust runtime with zero `unsafe` that cross-compiles to Cortex-M4F. It turns raw sensor streams into compact typed signs and shares them between robots over a mesh that needs no GPS or base station. It sits under a robot's control policy as its representation layer.

**[grey-walter-tortoise](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/grey-walter-tortoise)**: W. Grey Walter's 1949 tortoise as heap-free C++ firmware, with a [browser simulation](https://bamicallef35-netizen.github.io/grey-walter-tortoise/) kept identical to the firmware by a parity test.

Smaller projects:

- [viab](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/viab): stigmergic construction after the TERMES robots and R&Sie(n)
- [colloquy](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/colloquy): Gordon Pask's conversational machines, in the browser
- [viable-system-model](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/viable-system-model): Stafford Beer's VSM as a typed, tested Python library
- [offline-edge-node](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/offline-edge-node): local chat, retrieval and vision with network access blocked at runtime
- [random-mode](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/random-mode) and [humours](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/humours): generative architecture after François Roche, exported as drawing sheets
- [responsive-devices](https://github.com/bamicallef35-netizen/responsive-devices): six speculative devices and eight keyring sensors, each with a parts list

Walter, Ashby, Pask and Beer all asked what a system needs in order to keep itself going. François Roche put that question to buildings in his practice (R&Sie(n), later New-Territories), treating architecture as a protocol run under uncertainty. I'm writing a book on his work. Autonomy without GPS asks it of a vehicle.
